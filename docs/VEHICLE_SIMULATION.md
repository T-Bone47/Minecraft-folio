# The Drive

`lib/frontier/drive.ts` integrates a bicycle-style teaching model at 60 fixed simulation steps/second. Units are metres, seconds and radians. Acceleration is 8 m/s² before drag; top forward speed is capped at 22 m/s, reverse at 5 m/s. Steering interpolates toward a speed-dependent limit. Braking removes speed without changing direction; drag and rolling resistance oppose motion. The browser caps catch-up at 0.1 seconds to avoid a spiral after backgrounding.

The fictional perimeter circuit has centre radius 45 metres, half-width 2.5 metres and vehicle clearance 0.8 metres. Radial boundary contact projects the car inside the barrier and dissipates speed. The circuit was moved beyond all facility walls to prevent the old track/building intersections. This is not professional F1 physics; it has no tyre, aero, suspension or gearbox simulation. Gear/RPM and shift-light readings are omitted until supported by a model.

Start at (-45, 0), heading +Z. A/D steer, W accelerates, S brakes then reverses, Space brakes, C switches chase/cockpit (configurable to B; V is an alias), R resets, E exits. A lap requires four consecutive quarter-circle gates based on unwrapped angular progress. Reversing loses progress; stopping and shortcutting cannot finish a lap. Four sector intervals sum to lap time. Gate timing has fixed-step resolution, not millisecond measurement accuracy.

The reference is generated by a feedback controller using the same integrator and gate logic. It is reproducible, not a professional target or external benchmark. The browser debrief compares sectors and speed by progress and exposes the actual JSON samples. Sampling is bounded to 6,000 points (10 minutes at 10 Hz); long sessions therefore truncate the captured trace.
